# Weight survey of the kraken model zoo (read-only)

Written 07.11.2026 on asteraix for the manuscript (`paper/manuscript.md`, Section 6.4; the brief calls it 6.5). Brief: `docs/BRIEF_ZOO_SURVEY.md`.
Code: `wsp/zoo.py` (survey), `scripts/zoo_fetch.py` (download), `scripts/zoo_report.py` (tables), `wsp/viz.py zoo` (figures).
Data: `docs/zoo/survey.json` (everything below), `docs/zoo/fetch.json` (DOI and sha256 of every file downloaded), `docs/zoo/gateway_zenodo.json`
(the gateway's Zenodo-sourced entries), `docs/zoo/tables.md` (the tables of this file, regenerated by `scripts/zoo_report.py`).
No forward pass, no image, no held-out data; kraken 7.0.2 on CPU; model files stay in `models/zoo/` (git-ignored).

## 0. What was surveyed, and what was not

- **30 of the gateway's 37 third-party kraken models were downloaded and read.** The brief and `docs/MODELS.md` say 42; the gateway
  (`gateway_models`, 63 entries) lists 38 entries with a Zenodo DOI, 37 of them kraken models and one the `party` base model. The YAML
  itself is not on this machine, so the count is the gateway's. I did not find 42.
- **7 were not obtained.** `kraken-gallicorpora` (10.5281/zenodo.7410529) was refused by Zenodo with `HTTP 429 TOO MANY REQUESTS`
  (`https://zenodo.org/oai2d?…verb=GetRecord`). Following the brief ("if a record is refused, stop and report"), I stopped the downloads there and
  did not retry. The six not attempted afterwards: `openiti_persian_print`, `openiti_ottoman_print`, `openiti_arabic_print`, `lectaurep_french`,
  `hebrew_sephardi`, `english_print`. The two OpenITI children therefore have a documented base (gateway `base_model` = the OpenITI Arabic model)
  that cannot be tested.
- **All 30 loaded under kraken 7.0.2 without error.** Alongside them 10 in-house files from `models/kraken/` were read: the six models of the article's Table 1 (four Bern, two German XIX) and four comparators
  (`medieval-shard00-std` with the standard stack, `plus-shard00-cr1`, `medieval-shard00-v1`, `collapse-probe-131-cr1`; the `Cr255` probe is left out as in the readings). The seventh model
  already read, the parent `cursive_inzigkofen`, is the zoo file; the in-house copy is byte-identical (sha256 equal).
- The survey is one snapshot of what the gateway serves, not of the kraken repository, and says nothing about the zoo's 48 models in 18 writing systems
  that the manuscript mentions.

## 1. Method

1. Each model is loaded with `KrakenSubject` (CoreML `.mlmodel`; every one of the 40 is). Reported: VGSL string with block names, dropout rates and
   output width removed (the *architecture key*), tensor and parameter counts, codec size, and the codec split by Unicode category of its first code point into
   Latin letters, digits, punctuation/symbols, combining marks and other (non-Latin letters, spaces, …). Metadata keys of the file and the README/`metadata.json` text
   of the Zenodo record were searched for sentences naming a base model; no `.mlmodel` carries a key that names a base or training data (the metadata keys are
   `accuracy, metrics, seg_type, one_channel_mode, model_type, codec, hyper_params, vgsl`, sometimes `legacy_polygons`, `completed_epochs`), so
   **a documented base exists only in the gateway field `base_model` and in README text**, and I quote which of the two.
2. Output layer (`O_*`): row norms, the blank row (row 0): norm, bias, and the bias **relative to the mean of the other biases**
   (a constant added to all biases leaves the softmax unchanged, so only the relative value can matter; I report both). The ten most frequent letters are a fixed proxy,
   `e n i a r t s o u d` (no corpus is read); they are tested only on models whose codec contains all ten.
3. Models with the same architecture key form a group. In a group, the distance of two models is ‖a − b‖ / mean(‖a‖, ‖b‖) over all non-output tensors
   (conv, BiLSTM; symmetric); independent random networks sit at √2 ≈ 1.41. For a parent → child pair the per-tensor relative L2 and cosine are computed as in
   `wsp.stats.lineage_diff`, with output rows **aligned by character** (`wsp.align.codec_row_map`), never by index.
4. Documented pairs are those of the gateway `base_model` or a README sentence (T3); the in-house pairs are from `docs/MODELS.md`. A pair is tested only if
   the architectures agree and the weights are related (distance < 1.0); otherwise the table says so instead of reading it.
5. The three observations of `docs/READINGS.md` are turned into counted criteria (T5–T7). *Rare characters* are not measured on a corpus; the proxy is
   "outside basic Latin letters, digits, space and ASCII punctuation" (about two thirds of every codec), so observation 1 is tested in direction, not in frequency.

**What the two tables say.**

- **The standard stack is the zoo's majority.** 21 of the 30 zoo models (70 %) have exactly the stack of the in-house models
  (`Cr3,13,32 … Lbx200 … O1c<N>`, 4.0–4.1 M parameters, group A; the in-house models add 7 more: the six of Table 1 and `medieval-shard00-std`). Their dropout is written in two ways in the
  VGSL strings (`Do0.1,2` or `Do.{…}1,2`), which is no difference in weights. Nine zoo models are different:
  six use the group-normalised stack of CATMuS-Print (group B: `Cr4,2,32,4,2 Gn32 … Lbx256`, 5.7 M parameters), one is the same with a 48-pixel input
  (`estournelles_typewritten`, group C), and two are the 140-pixel `Cr4,16,32 … Lbx256` stack of TRIDIS (group D, 6.3 M). So all 30 fall into three families (standard stack 21, group-normalised stack 7 including the 48-pixel variant, TRIDIS stack 2); `kuzushiji`
  uses the standard stack with a 16,906-class output layer (10.8 M parameters), the only large codec.
- **Codec composition varies more than architecture.** Latin letters are 0–0.73 of the codec (median 0.38 among zoo models; `kuzushiji` and `samaritan` 0.00;
  `cyrillic_uncial` 0.08), combining marks 0 to 61. The CATMuS-lineage models carry 40–44 combining marks; `latin_expanded_11c` (75 classes) is the smallest Latin codec.
- **The blank row is a distinct row in every model.** Its norm is below the mean of the other rows' norms in 40 of 40 (T1, last column), and its bias is the
  highest in 27 of 40 (20 of 30 in the zoo).

## 3. Lineage: weights against the record

Figure 2 is the main result of this section: **in a group of 28, only three clusters of related weights exist, and almost everything else is as far apart as random
networks** (median pair distance 1.41). Of the 378 pairs of group A, the ones below 1.0 are the Bern family with `cursive_inzigkofen` (and, further out,
`catmus_medieval`, `bastarda_inzigkofen` and `old_norse_am305` at 0.6–0.9), `mccatmus`–`prima`, `bohemian_19th` with the two German XIX models, and (1.02) `glagolitic_print`–`samaritan`.

**Where the weights agree with the record.** `bastarda_inzigkofen` and `cursive_inzigkofen` (CATMuS Medieval, distance 0.72 and 0.74),
`textualis_inzigkofen` (TRIDIS v2, 0.33), `french_vietnamese` and `latin_incunabula` (CATMuS-Print Large, 0.19 and 0.33) and all six in-house pairs

Note the size of the move: the two Inzigkofen fine-tunes of CATMuS Medieval are far from it (0.9 pooled relative L2 on conv and BiLSTM, against 0.1–0.3 in the in-house pairs);
these are heavy retrainings that started from the parent rather than gentle adaptations.

**Where they do not, and one caution.**

1. **`bifrost_old_norse`** (gateway `base_model` CATMuS Medieval) is at 1.36 from `catmus_medieval`, as far as from unrelated models, and has no near neighbour at all.
   Its Zenodo text names no base. The weights do not support the documented base *as the file now in the zoo*; they do not say what it was trained from.
   One reading: another version of CATMuS Medieval; I cannot confirm or exclude that from metadata.
2. **`catmus_gothic_print`** (gateway `base_model` CATMuS Medieval; README: "fine-tuned on CATMuS Medieval") has a different architecture (group B, `Gn` stack,
   5.7 M parameters, against the 4.1 M standard stack of `catmus_medieval`); a weight-level fine-tune of this file is impossible, and the model is 1.40 from `catmus_print_large`,
   the other CATMuS print model, in the same group. The record can only refer to another file or to data, not to initial weights.
3. **`cursive_inzigkofen`** (caution, not a contradiction) is documented as a fine-tune of CATMuS Medieval but ranks its parent only 5th of 27 in distance (0.74): the four Bern models are closer to it (0.13–0.37),
   as they should be, and `old_norse_am305` (0.84) is about as close as the parent. The record is plausible; the ranking shows how far the fine-tune moved.

**Relations that no record names (T4).**

- **`mccatmus` → `prima`:** distance 0.26; `prima`'s 146-character codec contains all 116 characters of `mccatmus` at identical row indices. The weights and the codec say `prima` extends `mccatmus`
  (or both extend a third model that is not in the set); the Zenodo text of `prima` that I could read has no base sentence (the Zenodo record of `prima` has no text file among its files).
- **`bohemian_19th` → `german-xix-v1`:** distance 0.70; the 224-character codec of `german-xix-v1` contains all 135 characters of `bohemian_19th` at identical row indices.
  `docs/MODELS.md` lists the base of `german-xix-v1` as "to confirm"; this is a candidate for it, not a confirmation. It is not a plain fine-tune in the sense of
  the other pairs: the first conv block is nearly unrelated (relative L2 1.29, where the in-house pairs have 0.07–0.13) while C_3–C_9 are at 0.59–0.74. I read it as shared
  initialisation with a re-learnt first block, not as a documented fact.
- **`old_norse_am305` ↔ `catmus_medieval`** (0.61, the closest pair of the standard stack after the in-house ones): the README says "fine-tuned from a base model trained on AM 302 fol", a model that
  is not in the gateway list; its codec shares only 99 characters with CATMuS Medieval and none at the same row index (1 of 99). The base of the base is presumably in the CATMuS line; the weights agree, the codec was rebuilt.

### Reading of the observations

**(2) The blank bias — holds, and is stronger than the raw value shows.** Relative to the mean of the other biases the blank bias rises in 11 of 11 documented pairs (5 of 5 zoo pairs);
the raw bias rises in 10 of 11 (4 of 5; `french_vietnamese` −0.009). The reason the relative value is larger is that *the other biases fall*: their mean dropped in every pair
(by 0.05–0.60), so in the in-house readings "blank bias +0.06…+0.12" is, for the softmax, a rise of +0.16…+0.57. For the editing sessions this matters: setting
`bias[0]` back by its raw Δ undoes only part of the change in the quantity that decides the outputs. Across the 40 models without pairs: relative blank bias > 0 in 39 (only `estournelles_typewritten`, −1.10, is negative; it is also the only model
whose blank is last of 106 by bias), the highest bias in 27. The in-house relative values (T1): `hertwig-v1` +1.80, `hertwig-v2` +2.00, `schlussredaktion` +2.39, `combined` +2.58, `xix-v1` +2.99, `xix-blb` +3.15,
`shard00-std` +0.33 and the parent `cursive_inzigkofen` +1.48 (the zoo median is 1.18, the in-house median 1.90). Whether a pair's rise *causes* deletions is not something weights show.

**(1) Rare rows — holds in direction, not in selectivity, and fails for one pair.** Among the special rows, the norm grew in 10 of 11 pairs (zoo 4 of 5; the exception `latin_incunabula`
has only 51 shared special rows and 29 % grew) and the bias fell in a majority in 11 of 11; the median bias change of special rows is negative in every pair (−0.06 to −0.84) while that of the frequent letters is near 0 (−0.06 to +0.06).
But "the rows that moved most are the rare ones" holds in only 6 of 11 (zoo 3 of 5) by median movement and in 8 of 11 by the top decile, and all four criteria hold in 4 of 11 (**zoo 1 of 5**). In `tridis_v2` → `textualis_inzigkofen` and `catmus_print_large` → `french_vietnamese`
the frequent letters moved *more* than the special rows (0.17 against 0.10; 0.38 against 0.17): fine-tuning on a language with its own frequent letters moves those rows. The size of "30–110 % norm growth" is not reproduced:
the median norm ratio of special rows is 0.85–1.52 over the 11 pairs (1.41 and 1.52 for the two Inzigkofen models, 1.02 for TRIDIS → textualis); READINGS' 30–110 % referred to the rows that moved most, which are not tabulated here. The companion claim, that frequent letters have the weakest rows, holds without any pair:
the mean norm of the ten frequent rows is below that of all others in 37 of 37 models that contain the ten letters (median ratio 0.50), and at least five of the ten weakest non-blank rows are frequent letters in 26 of 37.
Because "rare" is a proxy here, a corpus-based test (frequency from the child's training lines) is the way to settle selectivity; this survey cannot.

**(3) Conv moves less than the recurrent stack and the output layer — not supported as stated.** Pooled over weights, the conv stack moved less than the BiLSTMs in 8 of 11 pairs (zoo 2 of 5) and less than the output rows in 4 of 11 (zoo 2 of 5);
in the heavy `catmus_medieval` retrainings the three are about equal (0.90, 0.88–0.90, 0.70–0.81), and in `textualis_inzigkofen` the conv is highest of the three. **What does hold is a depth pattern**: the first conv block moves least of the four in 11 of 11 pairs (5 of 5 zoo pairs; relative L2 0.05–0.26),
and the conv blocks increase with depth in 9 of 11. READINGS states the contrast with per-tensor means that include conv biases (tiny norms, large ratios) and the zero `bias_ih` of the LSTMs (counted as no movement); under that definition conv < BiLSTM in only 2 of 11 pairs, i.e. READINGS' own numbers
(conv blocks 0.07–0.12 against LSTM blocks 0.06–0.09 for `hertwig-v1`) point the other way from its sentence 3. The sentence should be restated as "the first conv block barely moves and the movement grows with depth".

### What the survey found that was not asked for

- **Large row norms:** `mendelssohn_letters`, `textualis_inzigkofen` and `tridis_v2` have mean output-row norms of 53–60 against a zoo median of 10.3; their blank bias ranks 1, 32 and 61 by bias despite relative values of +8.9, +7.5 and +6.8. Their norms are not comparable to the others'; I have no explanation. `kuzushiji` (16,906 classes) is a different regime again.
- **Models whose blank bias is hardly above the rest:** `latin_expanded_11c` (+0.10), `latin_graphematic_11c` (+0.11), `plus-shard00-cr1` (+0.09) and the collapse probe (+0.02): these have the weakest output rows too (mean norm 0.9–5.6). The collapse probe is the collapsed run of the readings (val accuracy 0.0083); I have no information on why the other three look alike.

## 7. What this means for Section 6.4 of the manuscript

The `[TODO]` of Section 6.4 can be replaced by a comparison that makes four statements, each supported by the tables above and no more. **First**, the standard stack is the zoo's majority: 21 of the 30 downloaded third-party
models (70 %) have exactly the architecture of the article's models, and all 30 fall into three families, so a weight-space reading of the article's kind is possible on most of the zoo (the survey covers 30 of the gateway's 37 kraken models; seven were not obtained). **Second**,
weights agree with the documented lineage where it can be tested (11 of 13 documented pairs, one clear contradiction, one architecture mismatch), and in a group of 28 models only three clusters are related at all; with a distance of ≈ 1.41 as the null, lineage is recoverable from weights alone for
fine-tunes that did not move far (0.1–0.3), and visible but far less clean for heavy retrainings (0.7–0.9). **Third**, the blank-bias observation generalises (relative to the other biases, 11 of 11 pairs, 5 of 5 independent zoo pairs), and should be
stated in the relative form; the article's raw "+0.06…+0.12" understates it. **Fourth**, the other two observations must be weakened in the text: rare-row inflation holds in direction (norm up, bias down for special rows) but not as "the rows that moved most", and not as a norm growth of 30–110 % (1 of 5 independent pairs meets all four criteria),
and the claim that the conv stack moves less than the recurrent stack and the output layer is not supported (conv < BiLSTM in 2 of 5 independent pairs, conv < output in 2 of 5); what the zoo supports is that the first conv block moves least and movement grows with depth. Section 6.3's sentence on this and READINGS' sentence 3 should be changed accordingly.
What the section cannot say: that the zoo's models were trained the way the in-house ones were, that "rare" is measured (it is a proxy), that any weight pattern predicts CER (nothing here was scored), or anything about the seven models not fetched. The five independent pairs come from three parents and three architectures, so counts of 5 are what they are.
