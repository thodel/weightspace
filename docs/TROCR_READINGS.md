# TrOCR track, stage 1: the family read as weights (11.11.2026)

Weights only: no forward pass, no image, no text, no trainer. Seven models of docs/MODELS_TROCR.md section A, fetched in stage 0 (docs/trocr/DIGESTS.json): `base` (`microsoft/trocr-base-handwritten`), `kurrent`, `xvi-xvii`, `from-kurrent-v2`,
`v2`, `full-v1`, `medieval` (`trocr-medieval-escriptmask`). The third-party fine-tunes named in the epic were **not** fetched (stage 0 forbade `extra`); that is a decision for Tobias (see the end).
Code: `wsp/trocr_family.py` (per-tensor readings), `scripts/trocr_readings_report.py` (tables), `scripts/trocr_displacement.py` (move directions, pooler identity). Data: `docs/trocr/readings.json` (every tensor × every pair),
`docs/trocr/readings_tables.md` (T1 to T9, regenerated by the report script), `docs/trocr/displacement.json`. Everything below is computed from those files.

## Method

Tensors are paired by canonical name (480 stored, 478 used by the forward; the ViT pooler is unused). Distance as in R5 (docs/ZOO_SURVEY.md): ‖a − b‖ / mean(‖a‖, ‖b‖), pooled over the tensors of a part.
**The kraken reference of 1.41 for unrelated networks does not apply:** every model here starts from the same base, so there is no unrelated pair to anchor a null. The null of stage 2 (the sibling pair, seeds) is the reference;
until then a distance is read against the other distances of the family, not against a threshold. The documented parent of each model is from the cards (docs/MODELS_TROCR.md), not from the weights.

## Findings

**1. Lineage is recoverable from weights, with a margin that depends on what is pooled (Gate 1: pass, with caveats).**

- *Rooted at `base`*, the documented parent is the nearest non-descendant for all six models when only weight matrices are pooled (T2; T1 numbers in the table): `kurrent` 0.228 to `base` against 0.230 to `v2`
  (a margin of 0.002), `medieval` 0.175 to `base` against 0.184 to `v2`, `full-v1` 0.140 to `xvi-xvii` against 0.154 to its sibling, `from-kurrent-v2` 0.088 to `xvi-xvii`, `xvi-xvii` 0.232 to `kurrent`, `v2` 0.090 to `base`.
  With all tensors pooled (T1, embeddings dominate) `medieval` is nearer to `v2` (0.235) than to `base` (0.258), and the minimum spanning tree has 4 of 6 documented edges (T3: `v2`–`kurrent` and `v2`–`medieval` take the place of `base`–`kurrent`, `base`–`medieval`;
  `v2`, 3 epochs from `base`, is a hub). **The pooling was chosen after seeing T1; the weights-only reading is therefore exploratory, and its two narrowest margins (0.002, 0.009) are not evidence.**
- *The encoder separates lineage much more cleanly than the whole model.* `from-kurrent-v2` – `xvi-xvii` 0.039, `from-kurrent-v2` – `v2` 0.163 (T7): a factor of four, for the same training data. The sibling pair is **not** converged by the data:
  `from-kurrent-v2` – `v2` is 0.347, as large as `v2` – `xvi-xvii` (0.333) or `from-kurrent-v2` – `base` (0.361). Three epochs on 54 k samples keep the memory of the starting point.
- *The unused ViT pooler is an exact lineage marker.* `base` has none (the hub file of `base` stores no pooler); `kurrent` has one with random initial values, and `xvi-xvii`, `from-kurrent-v2`, `full-v1` carry exactly the same weight matrix (share of identical values 1.0, 768 × 768);
  `v2` and `medieval` each have another (share 0). The bias is the same everywhere (zeros), which is why the pooled share of a pair that differs reads 0.5 in `displacement.json`. So the chain `kurrent → xvi-xvii → {from-kurrent-v2, full-v1}` is confirmed bit-exactly,
  and `v2` and `medieval` are shown not to descend from `kurrent`, independently of any distance.
- Nothing is frozen: no weight matrix, bias or norm shares more than a chance fraction of values with its parent (T8; the scattered equal values are float coincidences at the 0.000 level). Stage 0 already found no freezing in `training_args`.

**2. Where the movement is: the decoder, and within it the embedding, far more than the encoder (T4).** Child ← parent, pooled relative L2:

| | encoder | decoder | decoder without embedding | tied embedding part |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `kurrent` ← `base` (20 epochs, 293 k lines) | 0.109 | 0.512 | 0.279 | 0.829 |
| `xvi-xvii` ← `kurrent` (20 epochs) | 0.112 | 0.561 | 0.283 | 0.919 |
| `from-kurrent-v2` ← `xvi-xvii` (3 epochs, 54 k) | 0.039 | 0.295 | 0.111 | 0.520 |
| `full-v1` ← `xvi-xvii` (1 epoch, 450 k) | 0.063 | 0.422 | 0.177 | 0.746 |
| `v2` ← `base` (3 epochs, 54 k) | 0.039 | 0.190 | 0.116 | 0.310 |
| `medieval` ← `base` (20 epochs) | 0.081 | 0.475 | 0.219 | 0.796 |

Within the encoder, attention and FFN move alike (0.16 and 0.14 after 20 epochs); embeddings (patch, positions) and norms hardly move (0.014 to 0.026). Within the decoder the order is embedding ≫ FFN (0.52) > cross-attention (0.26) > self-attention (0.20) ≫ norms.
The transformer's analogue of "the stack that reads moves least, the stack that writes moves most" (kraken: convolution against recurrent and output) is therefore **encoder against decoder**: it holds, and by a larger ratio (3 to 5 times) than in the kraken zoo (R5: only 2 of 5 pairs).

**3. Depth (T5).** The encoder moves in a hump: least at layers 0 and 11, most at layers 4 and 5 (0.185 → 0.253 → 0.110 after 20 epochs), in every child, so the first layers are not the "generic front end" of the kraken reading, which moved least at the first block and grew with depth.
The decoder is nearly flat for 3-epoch models (0.117 to 0.144) and rises with depth for the 20-epoch models (0.25 at layer 2 to 0.39 at layer 11). The depth pattern depends on the length of the fine-tune; **whether the encoder hump survives a seed null is stage 2.**

**4. Token rows (T9).** In every pair all 50,265 rows of the tied embedding moved (none exactly zero), including tokens that cannot occur in German text; relative movement grows slightly with token id (kurrent ← base 0.73 in the first decile, 0.85 in the last; `xvi-xvii` ← `kurrent` 0.73 and 0.97).
That is the direction of the kraken "rare rows grow", but the training text is not known, so id is a proxy; the frequency test is stage 2. Because the output projection is tied, every row also receives a gradient as an output logit at every step, which is a mechanism for the uniform movement that does not exist in kraken.

**5. Do moves add up, and do they agree? (T6, `displacement.json`).** Cosine between two displacement vectors, pooled over a group:

| moves compared | encoder | decoder without embedding | tied embedding |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| two children of one parent: `base`→`kurrent` and `base`→`v2` | 0.209 | 0.136 | 0.416 |
| `base`→`kurrent` and `base`→`medieval` | 0.182 | 0.121 | 0.573 |
| `base`→`v2` and `base`→`medieval` | 0.203 | 0.123 | 0.515 |
| `xvi-xvii`→`from-kurrent-v2` and `xvi-xvii`→`full-v1` | 0.237 | 0.112 | 0.182 |
| consecutive steps: `base`→`kurrent`, `kurrent`→`xvi-xvii` | 0.070 | 0.024 | **−0.476** |
| `kurrent`→`xvi-xvii`, `xvi-xvii`→`from-kurrent-v2` | −0.007 | −0.018 | −0.288 |
| `kurrent`→`xvi-xvii`, `xvi-xvii`→`full-v1` | 0.007 | −0.019 | −0.309 |
| same data, different origin: `xvi-xvii`→`from-kurrent-v2`, `base`→`v2` | 0.077 | 0.032 | −0.025 |

Read: children of one parent move partly alike (0.1 to 0.24; the vectors have 86 M and 247 M entries, so chance is near 0), and most strongly in the embedding (0.4 to 0.6, probably the shared adaptation to German text and to a smaller token set in use);
**consecutive steps are orthogonal in the encoder and decoder (|cos| < 0.07) and the embedding step partly undoes the previous one (−0.29 to −0.48)**, so the chain is not a straight path (T6 ratios 0.58 to 0.71, where 0.71 is orthogonal steps and 1 a straight line).
The same training data from two origins moves the weights almost orthogonally (0.08 encoder, 0.03 decoder): **the data does not determine the move; the starting point and the run do.** That bears on any "move toward the child" donor argument: a parent–child direction exists, but a sibling's direction is only weakly informative about another sibling's.

## What this does for the later stages

- Stage 2 reference: the weights-only distances of the sibling pair (0.340 pooled weights, 0.347 all, encoder 0.163) are the size of the lineage effect, so the same-data null cannot separate "lineage" from "parent": seeds from one parent (stage 2b, the trainer) are needed for a null at the scale of a single fine-tune.
- Stage 3: the tied embedding is the largest mover; planted defects there change input and output together and are, in this reading, the part the real fine-tunes change most.
- Stage 4 donor rule: `xvi-xvii` is the natural donor for `from-kurrent-v2` (0.088 weights-only); for `trocr-kurrent` the parent `base` (0.228) is nearly as far as `v2` and `medieval`, so the donor list needs `seen_by` for every candidate.
- Subjects (decision 1, both): the same readings hold for the two subjects; nothing here separates them.

## Limits

- No null: no distance here is compared to seed noise; every claim of the form "survives" waits for stage 2. The two weights-only margins of 0.002 and 0.009 carry no weight.
- Only the seven core models; the third-party fine-tunes (Riksarkivet, LaMOP, medieval-data, kazars24, ...) are not read. Fetching them is outside stage 0's instruction (`extra`); it needs Tobias's yes.
- Relative distances pool tensors of very different sizes (the embedding has 52 M of 334 M values); the parts are shown apart for that reason.
- The cosine reading uses pooled vectors; per-layer cosines are not computed.
- No function (CKA) reading: that needs forward passes on probe lines and is a stage-1b option, not run.

## Gate 1

**Pass.** The lineage is recoverable from weights for the small fine-tunes: `v2` (0.090 from its parent, nearest), `from-kurrent-v2` (0.088, nearest), `full-v1` (0.140, against 0.154 to its sibling: a margin of 0.014); confirmed independently and exactly by the pooler marker.
It is less clean for the long fine-tunes and for the whole-model pooling. The negative result of the epic's wording does not apply. The readings go on as hypotheses to stage 2, not as findings.
