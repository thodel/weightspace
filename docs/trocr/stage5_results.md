**Reading, as written in advance (section 6 of the preregistration).** C4 matches or beats arm I on both subjects at every setting that was run: the stage-4 gain of a single interpolation is a gain that plain continued training on the same practice lines already exceeds (A −5.45 against −4.09; B −5.53 against −3.36), and that training on the whole train side exceeds by a wide margin (−9.4 and −11.3). So arm I's gain is not evidence that the donor carries something the subject's own data cannot give; what it measures is that the held-out hand is close to the train side's hand and the subjects are not adapted to it. The sibling average on A is "below" arm I (best −2.40 against −4.09, vs arm I +1.69 [+0.95, +2.45]): averaging with same-recipe siblings reaches about 60 % of arm I's gain, so about two thirds of arm I's effect is not an average-of-neighbours effect alone and the parent contributes something siblings do not, but C4 gets far beyond both. For B the ceiling comparators (toward models that have seen Kurrent) beat arm I (−9.4, −9.2), so B's arm I toward the English IAM base was a weak donor choice. Caveats: C4 trains on BLB lines, so a gain says nothing about lines outside the BLB hand; the intervals are those of one held-out half of 17 objects; the confirmation half is unread.
