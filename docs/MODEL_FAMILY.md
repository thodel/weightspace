# The served kraken family as networks — Track C (PLAN.md phase 6)

Started 07.11.2026. Question: the kraken models agentic_historian uses (the ATR
gateway's kraken engine, 43 models) — how do they compare *as neural networks*?

- **RQ1** How much of the models is identical, and could in principle have been frozen in training?
- **RQ2** Which layers differ, and how strongly?
- **RQ3** Can a network be stabilised by exchanging parts, e.g. from a more general to a more specific model?

## Material

`data/family/models.json` is the gateway's list (read with `gateway_models`, 07.11.2026): 43 kraken
models, 37 published on Zenodo and 6 trained in-house. Weights for all 43 are on asteraix: the 6
in-house models (`models/kraken/`), 4 Zenodo models the trainer keeps as bases on the share
(`training_folder/bases/`), and the other 33 downloaded from the Zenodo records the gateway names
(`python -m wsp.family fetch --apply`, 07.11.2026: 33 files, 608 MB, every file's checksum equal to
Zenodo's) into `~/atr-cache/wsp/family/zenodo/`. The serving box idhefix is not reachable from asteraix.

**Five architecture groups.** Two models compare element by element only if they have the same input
height and the same tensor shapes outside the output layer (`wsp.family.arch`; a different dropout rate,
which has no weights, does not split a group):

| group | models | architecture |
|---|---:|---|
| A | 33 | CATMuS/kraken default, height 120: `Cr3,13,32 Mp Cr3,13,32 Mp Cr3,9,64 Mp Cr3,9,64 S Lbx200 ×3 O` — blocks `C_0 C_3 C_6 C_9 L_12 L_14 L_16 O_18` |
| B | 6 | group-norm variant, height 120: `Cr4,2,32 Gn Cr4,2,64 Gn Mp Cr3,3,128 Gn Mp S Lbx256 ×3 O` — catmus_print_large, catmus_gothic_print, latin_incunabula, french_vietnamese, cremma_medieval, medieval_latin_french_abbreviated |
| C | 2 | default layout, larger: height 140, `Cr4,16,32 … Lbx256 ×3` — tridis_v2, textualis_inzigkofen |
| D | 1 | english_print (2019): height 48, two convolutions, one `Lbx100` |
| E | 1 | estournelles_typewritten: group B's layout at height 48 |

Lineages and distances below are always within a group; CKA (function) too.

## Method

Code: `wsp/family.py` (inventory, weights, function, report, fetch) and `wsp/exchange.py` (RQ3).
Tables: `docs/family/tables.md`, generated by `python -m wsp.family report`; numbers in
`docs/family/*.json`.

**Weight space (RQ1, RQ2).** For every pair and block: share of bit-identical parameters,
share within float tolerance (|Δ| ≤ 1e-6 + 1e-4·|a|), relative distance ‖B − A‖ / mean(‖A‖, ‖B‖),
cosine. `O_18` is compared on the characters both alphabets share (rows paired by character).
Structural zeros (kraken's LSTM input biases, 1,600 per layer, zero in every model) are excluded
from the identity count. Element-wise comparison means something only inside a **lineage**
(models fine-tuned from a common ancestor share their initialisation): between independently
trained networks hidden units are in arbitrary order. The body cosine (all blocks except the output)
is the lineage test. Over the 544 comparable pairs it falls into three bands: 496 pairs below 0.2
(at most 0.17: unrelated), 7 between 0.2 and 0.5 (distant relatives, below), 41 above 0.6 (every
recorded fine-tune is here); nothing lies between 0.5 and 0.6, where the cut-off sits.

**Function (RQ2 across lineages).** Linear CKA (Kornblith et al. 2019) between two models'
outputs of the same block on the same 120 probe lines (40 each from `blb-train-300`,
`bern-train-300`, `medieval-val-300`; train side only; seed 20261107); 2,000 sampled frames per
block. CKA is invariant to unit order and scale, so it compares unrelated models as well.
Dead filters: a convolution filter whose ReLU output is 0 on every frame of the probe lines.

**Exchange (RQ3).** For a specific model S and a more general relative G in the same lineage,
one block at a time becomes `S + β (G − S)` (β = 1: G's block transplanted; β = 0.5: half way),
everything else stays S. Each variant reads S's own material (the first set) and two others;
each reading is compared with S's unedited reading of the same lines (paired per-line bootstrap,
2,000 resamples, and the block statistics of docs/BLOCK_STATS.md). With G = S's parent, β = 1
undoes the fine-tune in that block alone: if S's own set does not move, that block need not
have been trained (RQ1, functional). Practice sets only; no held-out set is read.

### RQ1 — nothing trained stays identical; what is identical is dead

- Between unrelated models, no live parameter is identical. Within every lineage of every group, the only
  identical parameters sit in the first two convolution layers (and in group B in the group norm between
  them): from 0 % (mccatmus → prima, catmus_medieval → old_norse_am305) up to 56–60 % (openiti
  ottoman ↔ persian, tridis ↔ textualis), and **0.0 % in every layer from the third on**. The body as a
  whole: below 0.5 % identical.
- These are **dead units**, not frozen learning: the unchanged first-layer filters output 0 on every
  probe frame (ReLU never fires, so no gradient reaches them), and the unchanged second-layer weights are
  exactly those that read the dead channels. german-xix-v1 → xix-blb-v1: 8 of 32 `C_0` filters dead and
  unchanged, and 25.5 % of `C_3` unchanged ≈ 8/32 of its input channels; openiti ottoman/persian: 19 of 32
  dead, 56–57 % unchanged.
- Dead first-layer filters are common across the family: 11 of the 43 models have none, 11 have a third
  or more (up to 19 of 32 in openiti_ottoman_print, openiti_persian_print and textualis_inzigkofen,
  18 in mendelssohn_letters). Along a fine-tune they mostly accumulate: catmus_medieval 0 → cursive 3 →
  hertwig-v2 6 → schlussredaktion-v1 7; openiti_arabic_print 11 → persian 19; tridis_v2 15 → textualis 19.
  A few revive (at most 1 per step in the Bern chain, 2 from bohemian_19th to german-xix-v1): "dead" here
  means silent on the 120 probe lines, and a filter that fires on the training material does get a gradient.
- So no layer is identical enough to have been frozen *as trained*; the identical part is capacity that
  does nothing, a pruning candidate rather than a freezing one. Whether a layer *could* have been frozen
  without loss is a functional question, answered by the exchange grid (β = 1 to the parent), below.

### RQ2 — distance grows with depth; the front end is shared across the whole family

- **Weight space, within lineages:** every fine-tune moves the late layers more than the first. Small
  fine-tunes barely touch the first convolution (Bern chain 0.07–0.10, group B 0.05–0.08) and move the
  recurrent layers and output 2–4× more; large ones (CATMuS → Inzigkofen/Bastarda, OpenITI Arabic →
  Persian/Ottoman) move the deeper layers by 0.5–0.85 and still the first layer least (0.24–0.56). One exception: bohemian_19th → german-xix-v1
  moved `C_0` the most of all layers (0.92 against 0.60–0.78 elsewhere), which suggests differently
  prepared input images (binarisation, normalisation or polarity) — to check in the trainer's record.
- **Function:** CKA falls with depth in every group and every relation class.

  | group, pairs | first conv | last conv | first LSTM | last LSTM | output |
  |---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
  | A, same lineage (37) | 0.85 | 0.71 | 0.62 | 0.39 | 0.19 |
  | A, distant relatives (7) | 0.93 | 0.61 | 0.56 | 0.22 | 0.03 |
  | A, unrelated (484) | 0.80 | 0.48 | 0.48 | 0.23 | 0.04 |
  | B, same lineage (3) | 1.00 | 0.95 | 0.79 | 0.41 | 0.32 |
  | B, unrelated (12) | 0.99 | 0.72 | 0.60 | 0.30 | 0.09 |

  Independently trained models compute much the same first layer — in group B practically the same
  (0.99) — and diverge in the recurrent stack; the last LSTM and the output layer are where models are
  individual. The front end is generic in function even where it shares no weights (unrelated pairs:
  cosine below 0.2, CKA 0.80 in the first layer). Full per-layer means and every pair: `docs/family/tables.md`,
  `docs/family/functional.json`.

### RQ3 — exchange: half-way moves in the front end stabilise a little; the recurrent stack trades

First grid, 07.11.2026, on the in-house lineages 1 and 2: five specific ← general pairs from lineages 1 and 2, every block at β = 0.5 and
1, each variant read on three practice sets (240 readings, CPU). Full tables: `docs/family/tables.md`;
numbers and block statistics per variant: `docs/family/exchange-*.json` (the per-line readings stay on
asteraix under `docs/family/readings/`, git-ignored, 12 MB).

**A caveat that bounds everything below.** For bern-hertwig-v2 and german-xix-blb-v1 the first set is
their own (train-side) material; for cursive_inzigkofen (`medieval-val-300`) and german-xix-v1
(`blb-train-300`) it is only the nearest material on asteraix. The "other" sets are read badly by every
model (CER 51–77 %), so a gain there is a gain on material these models cannot really read. A better
test of stabilisation needs out-of-domain sets each model reads at, say, 15–30 % CER.

- **RQ1, functionally: no block's fine-tune can be undone for free; the first convolution comes
  closest.** Transplanting the parent's block (β = 1) into german-xix-blb-v1 costs +0.27 points on its own
  set for `C_0`, +0.45 for `C_3` and +0.68 for the output layer, but +4.1 to +6.4 for each recurrent layer.
  Half-way (β = 0.5), `C_0` costs nothing (−0.02, interval includes 0) — in bern-hertwig-v2 too
  (+0.16 towards cursive_inzigkofen). So the first layer's fine-tune is at most half necessary:
  `C_0` (and in the Kurrent lineage `C_3`) are the freezing candidates; the recurrent layers are not.
- **Stabilisation without cost exists, but small and only in the convolutional blocks.**
  bern-hertwig-v2 with `C_9` half-way to cursive_inzigkofen: own set +0.18 (interval includes 0),
  medieval −1.08 and BLB −1.11 points (both excluding 0). With `C_3` half-way: own +0.30, medieval −2.24.
  german-xix-blb-v1 with `C_6` half-way to german-xix-v1: own +0.33, medieval −0.95.
- **The recurrent layers trade one for one or worse.** Moving `L_12`/`L_14`/`L_16` half-way towards the
  general model lowers CER on the other sets by 0.2–3.1 points and raises it on the model's own set by
  1.2–4.4 points. That is the fine-tune being undone, not stabilisation.
- **Only towards a better general model.** Nearly every exchange from bohemian_19th into german-xix-v1 makes
  german-xix-v1 worse, on all three sets (bohemian_19th reads them worse; the one exception is `L_14`
  half-way, −0.87 on `bern-train-300` at +1.69 on its first set). cursive_inzigkofen improves on
  all three sets with almost any block half-way to catmus_medieval (e.g. `L_16`: −2.8, −2.2, −1.6), because
  catmus_medieval reads all three better (48–54 % vs 67–77 %).
- **The output layer is no lever.** Exchanging `O_18` on the shared characters costs or does nothing in
  every pair (β = 1: +0.3 to +9.0 points on the first set).

- **latin_incunabula is the cleanest case so far**, because it reads its own material at 3.8 % and the outside
  sets at 25–29 %, where a change means something.
  - *Freezable (RQ1, functional):* the whole front end can be set back to the parent's with no measurable cost
    on its own material — `C_0` +0.06, `C_2` +0.16 and all three group norms +0.03 to +0.15 at β = 1, every
    interval including 0. Only the last convolution (`C_5` +0.45) and the recurrent layers (+2.5 to +2.9) carry
    the fine-tune. In this fine-tune the convolutional front end could have been frozen.
  - *Free stabilisation:* the first two group norms transplanted from catmus_print_large (β = 1): own set
    +0.05 / +0.03 (interval includes 0), Fraktur −0.28 / −0.23 and NewsEye −0.57 / −0.47 (both excluding 0).
    The output layer half-way: own +0.05 (includes 0), NewsEye −1.58.
  - *Recurrent layers trade, at a good rate here:* `L_11` half-way costs +0.29 on Reichenau for −1.38 on Fraktur
    and −2.97 on NewsEye.
- **Towards a better general model, nearly everything helps.** catmus_print_large reads all three print sets
  better than french_vietnamese; moving `C_2`, the LSTMs or the output layer half-way lowers CER on all three

  (−2.9) and loses heavily on the others (+10.5, +7.0): the last recurrent layer is where textualis_inzigkofen's
  specialisation sits.

**The candidate, held out (docs/RESULTS.md R4).** The stabilised latin_incunabula, scored once on 4,374 held-out
Reichenau lines (60 train-shard pages no practice or confirmation line came from): 4.99 % against 5.01 % unedited,

**Which candidate to enable (docs/RESULTS.md R9, pre-registered).** On held-out Fraktur pages both candidates read

**Answer so far:** the shared front end (RQ2) is where exchange can stabilise without cost (RQ3): half-way
in the default architecture, fully in the group-norm architecture, where the front end is functionally the
same across all six models (CKA 0.99): for latin_incunabula, two group norms plus the output layer half-way
stabilise it on print it was not trained for by 0.6–2.4 points, at no cost on its own material, confirmed on
fresh lines. Freezability is not additive: every front-end block alone could be reverted, the whole front end
together could not. The individuality of a model sits in its recurrent stack, which an exchange can only
trade against its own material — at a poor rate in the in-house fine-tunes, at a good one (about 1 : 5 to
1 : 10) for latin_incunabula. Exchange only helps towards a general model that reads the other material better.

## RQ3 where it matters: out-of-domain sets, combined moves, cross-lineage exchange (08.11.2026)

The first grid measured stabilisation on sets every model reads at 51–77 % CER. This round looks for material
*outside* each in-house model's training that the model reads at 15–30 %, tests the combined moves the first grid
suggested against own-material cost, scores pre-registered candidates on held-out data once, and tries exchange

### Part 3 — cross-lineage exchange after aligning units

`wsp/permute.py`, `scripts/align_exchange.py`. Per layer, units of S and G are matched by activation correlation on the 120
Track C probe lines (Hungarian assignment; conv channels over all positions, LSTM units per direction), G's weights are
permuted consistently (conv rows and next-layer input channels; `L_12`'s 960 inputs as `h·64 + c`, checked numerically;
LSTM gate rows, recurrent columns and next-layer inputs per direction; output columns), and **the permuted G reads every line
of every set exactly as G does** (899 of 899 and 599 of 599 lines identical). Then S takes G's aligned conv blocks up to `C_k`
(`front<k>`), the same with the last block's channels rescaled to S's activation scale (`front<k>s`), `C_0` half-way in aligned
coordinates (`C_0h-al`), and the unaligned transplant as control (`-raw`).

| pair | matched unit correlation, `C_0` … `L_16` (unaligned) | `C_0` aligned | `C_0` ½ aligned | whole front end aligned / rescaled / unaligned |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| blb-v1 ← german-xix-v1 (same lineage: control) | 0.65–0.83 (same; the matching is the identity on every unit from `C_3` on and on 25 of 32 `C_0` filters, where S has 9 dead) | own +0.23, federal **−1.96** | own +0.07, federal **−2.02** | own **+2.57** / **+2.33** / — |
| blb-v1 ← McCATMuS (unrelated, body cosine 0.04) | 0.21–0.36 (0.00–0.04) | CER 100 % on all sets | own **+8.7**, federal **+21.1** | CER 81 % / 46–63 % / 99–100 % |
| hertwig-v2 ← McCATMuS (unrelated, body cosine 0.06) | 0.18–0.36 (0.00–0.06) | CER 100 % on both sets | own **+2.58**, Schlussredaktion +0.61 | CER 39–53 % / 37–50 % / 98 % |

- **The method is sound:** the permutation is exactly function-preserving, and within a lineage the matching finds the
  identity and the aligned transplant reproduces the element-wise one.
- **Across lineages there is nothing to transplant one unit at a time.** Unrelated models have similar first layers as
  *spaces* (CKA 0.80) but not as *units*: the best one-to-one matching reaches a correlation of 0.2–0.4 per unit, against
  0.65–0.9 within a lineage. Any transplant costs: the first convolution alone takes CER to 100 %, half-way it costs +2.6 (hertwig-v2) to +21 points (blb-v1), the whole front end +20 to +67;
  alignment and rescaling reduce the damage but leave the model unusable. McCATMuS is also no better reader of this material
  (35–60 %). No candidate; nothing read held-out.
- What would be needed instead is a map that is not a permutation (a linear map between the two front ends' feature spaces,
  fitted on probe lines, folded into S's `C_3`) — a learned adapter, i.e. gradient-free regression rather than exchange.

### Answer to RQ3, as of 08.11.2026

Within a lineage, half-way or full exchange of the first convolution stabilises a specific model on out-of-domain material that
the more general parent reads better, at no measured own-material cost; on held-out documents the gain is real but small
(−0.35 to −1.07 points on sets read at 16–27 %), much smaller than practice suggested, because the practice gain sat in a few
documents. Combining further conv blocks only adds own-material cost. Towards a parent that reads the out-of-domain material
worse (the Bern lineage), exchange does not stabilise. Across lineages, unit alignment by permutation is exact but finds no
unit-level correspondence to exchange.
