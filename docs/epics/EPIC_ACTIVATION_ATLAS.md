# Epic: an activation atlas of a kraken recogniser: which units respond to which part of a line

Proposed 7 November 2026. Status: L0–L3 done; Gates 1 and 2 fail, so L4 is not run (see the closing synthesis of docs/ATLAS_REPORT.md, 15 November 2026). Owner: Tobias Hodel. Related: docs/MODEL_FAMILY.md (CKA, dead filters, exchange),
docs/READINGS.md, docs/SEED_NULL.md (five seed runs), `KrakenSubject.activations` in wsp/kraken_subject.py, docs/epics/EPIC_LLM_PLAYBOOK.md.

## Question

Can we draw the whole network (4 convolutional blocks, 3 BiLSTM layers of 200 units each way, a linear output layer over about 100 characters
and the CTC blank) and show, for a text line, which units are active where, which characters and parts of a line they stand for, and which of these
assignments are causal and stable rather than a picture?

## Verdict of the feasibility check (7 November 2026)

**Feasible on CPU, with the model and data we already have; the figure is easy, the claim behind it is the hard part.**

What makes it cheap:

- `KrakenSubject.activations(lines, blocks)` already returns the output of any named block (`C_0`, `C_3`, `C_6`, `C_9`, `L_12`, `L_14`, `L_16`, `O_18`) per line as frames x features, with the transforms of the recogniser. The model has 4.1 M parameters; a line is 120 px high and a few hundred frames wide (one frame is about 8 px of the line).
- The alignment from frame to character exists in the CTC output (emission spans), and per-character rows, dead filters and CKA between models are already computed (docs/family/).
- Five seeded runs of the same recipe exist (docs/seed-null/), which gives the test of whether a unit-level finding belongs to the data or to one random initialisation.

What makes the claim hard, and what the epic has to design around:

1. **"Where in the line" is not one question.** The convolutional receptive field is large (about 140 px by our own calculation, not yet verified) and the BiLSTM layers see the whole line in both directions, so the activation at frame t is not evidence located at frame t. We must separate *where a unit is active* (read from the activation), *what in the image it depends on* (occlusion and gradient tests) and *what the output depends on it for* (ablation).
2. **Units are not interpretable by default.** In the recurrent layers a character is typically spread over many units, and there is no unit correspondence between two models (only CKA-like similarity). A unit-by-unit story for one model is a description of that model, not of recognisers.
3. **Pictures persuade more than they prove.** Without controls (a randomly initialised network, shuffled lines, a seed run) any atlas looks meaningful.

## Levels of the atlas

- **L0 Activation map.** For a line: heatmaps of each block (units x frames) beneath the line image, the CTC emission track and the decoded text; selectable per unit group (conv channel, LSTM direction). A viewer, not a claim.
- **L1 Selectivity.** For each unit: the characters, character classes (ascender, descender, round, space, punctuation) and positions (word start, inside, end, line margin) it distinguishes, measured on a few thousand lines with AUROC and with a sparse linear probe per block; the same per direction in the BiLSTMs. Output: a table and a unit card per unit.
- **L2 Dependence on the image.** Receptive field per block measured by occlusion (slide a masked window over the line, record change of the unit and of the output); verifies or corrects the 140 px figure; per-character occlusion sensitivity map for the whole line; one gradient-based attribution method for CTC (the literature in docs/RELATED_WORK.md names several) checked against occlusion.
- **L3 Causal role.** Ablate (zero or mean) a unit, a channel, a group or a whole block and measure per-character change of CER on held-out lines; the units with the largest effects per character form the "circuit" of that character; compare with the dead filters already known and with the exchange moves that stabilised a model.
- **L4 Across models.** The same atlas for the family: how a fine-tune changes selectivity (parent against child, in the order of the lineage in docs/ZOO_SURVEY.md), and how much changes between five seeds (the null). Unit matching across models only through activation similarity, and reported as such.

## Deliverables

1. `wsp/atlas.py`: extraction (batching, caching to `~/atr-cache/atlas/`, never into the repository), selectivity, occlusion, ablation, plus tests on a small synthetic network.
2. A static viewer (`docs/atlas/index.html`, one JSON per model and line set, no server) with the line, the block heatmaps, the emission track and unit cards; generated by `scripts/atlas_build.py`.
3. Tables and figures for one Kurrent model (`xix-blb-v1`) and one Bern model (`hertwig-v2`) with five seed runs of the latter as the null.
4. `docs/ATLAS_REPORT.md` with the controls and the answers to the questions below; a short section for the article (Section 6 or a follow-up).

## Questions the report must answer

- Is the convolutional front end already character-selective, or only stroke-selective, and where does selectivity appear first (block by block)?
- Do BiLSTM units code characters, positions, word boundaries or context, and does one direction carry what the other does not?
- Does the picture of the unit cards of one seed run reappear in another seed run (stability), and is it greater than in a randomly initialised model?
- Do the units that matter in ablation coincide with those with high selectivity, with large weight changes in fine-tuning (docs/READINGS.md), or with the 'dead' filters?
- Does the atlas explain a known failure, for example the deleted spaces and `e` whose margin is decided upstream of the output layer (docs/CONTROLS_REPORT.md section 8)?

## Controls, rules and stop gates

- Controls for every selectivity or ablation claim: a randomly initialised network of the same architecture; the same analysis on lines with shuffled characters or on blank crops; five seed runs; units chosen on one set of lines and tested on a disjoint set (calibration, selection and evaluation lines are different pages).
- Intervals page-clustered; effects below one point of CER reported as inconclusive; ablation uses the same preregistered protocol file as the sessions (`docs/PROTOCOL.md`).
- CPU only, card 1 never; nothing written to the share, registry, Hugging Face or the trainer; weights and activation caches stay in the cache directory.
- Gate 1 (after L0 and L2): occlusion must reproduce the character positions the CTC alignment gives; if not, the viewer shows activation only and does not claim location.
- Gate 2 (after L1 and L3): if selectivity is not above the random-network and seed controls, the atlas is published as description only, without a 'circuit' claim.
- Gate 3: L4 only for pairs where Gate 2 passed.

## Risks and costs

Distributed codes mean few unit cards with a clean story; the viewer can mislead (every card carries its control value); ablation of LSTM units can break the whole sequence, so group ablations must be reported together with the effect of ablating random groups of equal size. Effort: L0 and L2 about two to three days of a session, L1 and L3 two more, L4 one; no GPU required.

## Relation to the other epics

The atlas is the evidence base for the LLM playbook (docs/epics/EPIC_LLM_PLAYBOOK.md): unit-to-character maps and causal ablation tables would turn the playbook's signature table from tensor statistics into statements about function. It should not block it: the playbook can start with the readings we already have.

## Acceptance

A viewer and a report that state, with the controls, what the first convolution, the later convolutions and each recurrent layer respond to along a line, how much of it is stable across seeds, and which causal claims survive ablation, together with the cases where the atlas shows nothing.
